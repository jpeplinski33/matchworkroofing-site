Local Knowledge

Homes & Roofs in Upper Arlington

Upper Arlington is known for its 1920s–1960s brick colonials, Tudors, and Cape Cods along tree-lined streets. These homes often have steeper pitches, multiple dormers, chimneys at the roof edge, and — on some of the oldest homes — original slate. Mature tree canopy means more debris in valleys and gutters, and older attics frequently need ventilation addressed as part of a replacement.

Chimneys & Dormers

Older UA homes put chimneys and dormers in the roof plane. Step and counter flashing at these intersections is where leaks start — and where craftsmanship shows.

Ventilation in Older Attics

Many pre-war and post-war attics were not designed for modern ventilation. Intake and exhaust should be evaluated together during replacement.

Color Against Brick

UA's red and buff brick reads differently against shingle color. We review physical samples against your brick and trim before you commit.

Frequently Asked Questions

My Upper Arlington home has an old slate roof. What are my options?

Some older UA homes still carry original slate. When slate reaches the end of its serviceable life, designer shingles such as Grand Manor offer a slate-look profile at a fraction of slate's cost and weight.

Do older homes need ventilation work during a roof replacement?

Often, yes. Intake and exhaust ventilation should be considered together, based on the attic and roof design. Your written proposal will note any ventilation changes.

Can you match the look of a historic Upper Arlington streetscape?

Designer shingle lines and copper accent details can complement traditional architecture. We review samples against your home's brick, stone, and trim.
