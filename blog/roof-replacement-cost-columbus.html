Cost & Planning

How Much Does a Roof Replacement Cost in Columbus, Ohio?

Homeowner guide · 6 min read

The short version: most Central Ohio architectural-shingle replacements land somewhere between the mid-four figures for a small, simple roof and well into five figures for a large, steep, or complex one. Any number quoted without looking at your roof is a guess — what matters is understanding what moves the number.

The Six Things That Set the Price

1. Roof size and pitch

Roofing is priced by the "square" (100 sq ft of roof surface). A ranch might be 18 squares; a large two-story with a complex roofline can exceed 35. Steeper pitches cost more: they slow the crew, require more safety equipment, and waste more material at cuts.

2. Existing layers

A tear-off of one layer is standard. Multiple existing layers add disposal weight and labor, and Ohio code limits how many layers can remain. Your proposal should state the assumed layer count and the price if more are found.

3. Decking condition

Rotted or delaminated decking must be replaced before shingles go on. No honest estimate can see decking under a roof that hasn't been torn off — what it can do is state the per-sheet price for repairs, so there are no open-ended extras.

4. Flashing, chimneys, and valleys

Step and counter-flashing at walls and chimneys, valley linings, and pipe boots are where roofs actually leak. Reusing old flashing is cheaper and worse. Copper details cost more and last longer.

5. Ventilation

Intake and exhaust should be evaluated together. Adding ridge vent or correcting intake adds cost and is often the difference between a shingle that lasts its warranty and one that doesn't.

6. Material tier

A standard architectural shingle (CertainTeed Landmark), a premium Max Def version (Landmark PRO), and a designer slate-look (Grand Manor) carry meaningfully different material costs — on the same roof, the tier alone can move the total by thousands.

Why "Price Per Square" Ads Mislead

Advertised per-square prices usually assume a walkable pitch, one layer, no decking repair, standard flashing, and a basic shingle. Real roofs have chimneys, valleys, and surprises under the old shingles. The useful question isn't "what's your price per square" — it's "what does your proposal include."

What a Real Proposal Looks Like

A written proposal should itemize the shingle line and accessories, underlayment and leak barrier, flashing scope, ventilation changes, decking repair pricing, protection and cleanup, and the total. If two bids differ, compare the scopes line by line — the cheaper bid is often just a shorter list.
