The Matchwork Standard

A Gutter Is a Drainage System, Not a Trim Piece

Gutters fail quietly: overflow behind the gutter rots fascia, short downspouts soak foundations, and undersized runs overflow in the storms that matter. We treat gutters as the last mile of the roof system.

Sizing & Pitch

A gutter only works if it is sized for the roof area above it and pitched to the downspouts. We size runs and place downspouts based on how your roof actually sheds water.

Downspout Discharge

Water has to leave the building. Downspout placement and extensions that carry discharge away from the foundation are part of the scope, not an accessory.

The Roof-Edge Connection

Drip edge and gutter apron direct water off the shingles and into the gutter — not behind it. Because we roof, this detail is handled in-house.

Overflow in moderate rain, staining on the fascia, sagging runs, pooling near the foundation, and landscaping washouts below the eaves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size gutters does my home need?

Most homes use 5-inch K-style gutters; larger or steeper roof planes often justify 6-inch gutters and larger downspouts. Sizing depends on the roof area draining to each run.

Are gutter guards worth it?

Under heavy tree canopy, guards reduce cleaning frequency and overflow risk. Performance varies by guard type and debris, so we recommend based on your trees and roof pitch.

Should gutters be replaced with the roof?

Not always — but drip edge, gutter apron, and gutter attachment interact with the roof edge. If gutters are near end of life, combining the work into one scope avoids disturbing a new roof edge later.
