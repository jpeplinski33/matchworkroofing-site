The Matchwork Standard

One Contractor for the Whole Detail

A chimney leak can be a roofing problem, a masonry problem, or both — and it is usually both. When the roofer and the mason are the same company, the flashing, the mortar joints, and the crown get fixed together, once.

Counter-Flashing Done Right

Counter-flashing should be cut into the mortar joint and turned down over the step flashing. Caulk-only flashing is a short-lived patch — and a common source of repeat leaks.

Chimney Crowns & Caps

The crown sheds water off the top of the chimney. Cracks let water into the brickwork, where freeze-thaw cycles do the damage.

Brick & Stone at the Roof Line

Tuckpointing, spalled brick replacement, and stone repair where masonry meets roofing — planned with the flashing scope, not separately.

Water staining on the ceiling near the chimney, white efflorescence on brick, crumbling mortar joints, and a cracked or missing crown.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my chimney leak even though the roof is new?

Chimney leaks usually trace to flashing or masonry, not the field shingles. Counter-flashing cut into the mortar joints — not caulked against brick — is the durable detail.

What is tuckpointing?

Tuckpointing removes deteriorated mortar from joints and packs in new mortar. On chimneys above the roof line, failed joints let water into the brickwork and behind the flashing.

Can you repair a chimney crown?

Yes. Cracked crowns let water into the chimney structure. Repair or rebuilding the crown is often paired with counter-flashing work so the whole detail is watertight.
